# Participant quick start

If you are unsure what to enter, use a predefined nudge beside the input field. You can edit the loaded text before using it.

Detailed prompts appear as a balanced one-view sheet on desktop. Use **Copy entire prompt** to copy the full source in one action, or download the prompt as a text file. On a narrow screen, the sheet becomes a single readable column.

AI Rx Live Demo Studio is a teaching environment. It shows how AI can assist with drafts, research orientation, communication, teaching, and workflow design while a healthcare professional remains responsible for the result. The practical rule is simple: patient first, doctor led, AI assisted.

## Before you begin

Do not upload a real patient name, voice recording, photograph, report, prescription, contact detail, hospital number, date of birth, or other identifying information. Use the fictional cases in the app.

For every task, ask six questions:

1. What patient-centred decision are we supporting?
2. Is the input safe, complete, and appropriate to upload?
3. What options does the AI suggest, and what evidence supports them?
4. What is missing or uncertain?
5. What is the safest next action?
6. Who validates, communicates, documents, and follows up?

## Three useful starting points

- **Prompt Library:** choose the task and specialty, read the complete prompt in one view, copy it, and replace only the marked patient and source fields.
- **AI vs Doctor Cases:** read the date, patient, procedure, and series information before the AI output. Then compare the AI’s perceived diagnosis with the doctor’s actual diagnosis.
- **Decision Support:** use the six checks before accepting or acting on an output.

## During a demo

Read the fictional input before looking at the generated output. Mark missing information. Check names, dates, measurements, medicines, doses, units, negations, laterality, series, and changes over time. Separate what the source states from what the AI infers. Open cited sources. Ask which tacit clinical cue could change the interpretation, and who is responsible for the final action.

## Diagnostic simulations

The eight modality groups contain abstract interface placeholders. They are not medical images, do not carry a diagnosis, and were not processed by a validated model. Their purpose is to practise image-quality review, intended-use questions, uncertainty, and human oversight.

## Take-away exercise

Choose one specialty case. Copy one complete prompt. Compare one AI answer with a doctor’s judgment. Write down what the model added, what it missed, what remained uncertain, and what patient-centred action a clinician should consider next.
